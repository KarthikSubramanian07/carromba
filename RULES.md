# The rules of Carromba

Carrom has a dozen regional rulebooks and no two front-porch games agree on all of
them. So Carromba picks one clear set and sticks to it. It is based on the
International Carrom Federation laws, trimmed to what one player at a screen
actually needs, with every fiddly case pinned down so the game never has to shrug
and guess. This is the exact ruleset the code enforces. If you ever wonder why the
board did something, the answer is here.

## The board

Nineteen discs to start. One red Queen dead center, then eighteen men packed
around her in two rings, nine white and nine black, colors alternating. You shoot
with a striker: bigger and heavier than a man, and it lives on your baseline.

There are four pockets, one in each corner. Sink your men, leave your opponent's
alone, deal with the Queen, and clear the board.

## Taking a shot

Set the striker anywhere along your baseline, between the two circles. Pull back
like a slingshot and let go. The harder you pull, the harder it goes. You must
send the striker into the pack: catch nothing at all and it is a foul (see below).

## Whose men are whose (the open board)

The board starts "open," with no colors assigned. The first player to sink a man
claims that color for the rest of the board, and the other color goes to the
opponent. Sink a white first and the whites are yours. Simple as that.

## Keep shooting, or hand it over

Pocket at least one of your own men on a stroke, with no foul, and you shoot again.
Miss, or only move the pack around, and the turn passes.

Pocket one of your opponent's men and it stays down, counting toward their color.
It does not win you the turn and it is not a foul. It just helps them, so aim
carefully.

## The Queen

The red Queen is worth chasing, but she comes with a string attached: you have to
**cover** her.

- You may pocket the Queen at any time.
- To keep her, sink one of your own men on the **same stroke** or the **very next
  stroke**. That is the cover.
- Sink the Queen and one of your own men together and she is covered on the spot.
- Sink the Queen alone and you keep shooting, but your next stroke has to land one
  of your men or she goes back to the center circle, uncovered, for anyone to try
  again.
- The Queen is never kept on a foul stroke. If you foul while she is in play, she
  returns to the center.

(The strict ICF laws also say you cannot pocket the Queen until you have opened
your own color. Carromba drops that one on purpose, because "pocket anytime, but
cover her" is friendlier for a quick game and still keeps the Queen honest.)

## Fouls

A foul ends your turn. As a penalty, one of the men you have already pocketed comes
back to the center circle (if you have one to give). These are the fouls:

- **Striker pocketed.** The striker goes down a pocket. Turn over, one man returned.
- **Touched nothing.** The striker never reaches a man. A wasted stroke is a foul.
- **Finishing before the Queen.** You cannot pocket your last man while the Queen is
  still unsettled. The man comes back, and if you were holding the Queen for a
  cover, she comes back too. Deal with the Queen first.
- **Pocketing your opponent's last man.** You do not get to end the game for them.
  Their man returns to the center and the turn passes.

## Winning

Clear all of your own men, with the Queen covered, and the board is yours. Because
of the "finish before the Queen" rule, the Queen is always settled by the time
anyone's last man drops, so a win is always clean.

That is the whole game. Now go break the pack.
